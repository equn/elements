## 1. Hydrogen (H) · 氢 (qīng)

- **Chinese:** 氢 — *qīng* · radical 气 (gas)  
- **Category:** Reactive nonmetal  
- **Atomic number:** 1 · **Atomic mass:** 1.008  
- **State at room temperature:** gas  
- **Charge numbers — common:** +1  
- **Charge numbers — all known:** -1, +1  
- **Discovered:** Henry Cavendish, 1766  
- **Name means:** From Greek hydro + genes, 'water maker'  
- **Abundance in Earth's crust:** 1400 ppm  

**Description**

Hydrogen is the smallest and lightest atom in the whole universe: just one proton with one electron whizzing around it. It is also the most common element anywhere — about three quarters of all the stuff in space is hydrogen. Stars like our Sun are giant balls of hydrogen squeezing together to make light and heat.

**Fun facts**

- If the periodic table were a race, hydrogen would win for being first AND lightest.
- The Sun burns about 600 million tons of hydrogen every single second.
- Two hydrogen atoms plus one oxygen atom make water — so you are mostly made of hydrogen by atom count.
- Hydrogen gas is so light that a balloon filled with it floats up and away.
- Rockets use liquid hydrogen as fuel because it packs a huge punch for its weight.

## 2. Helium (He) · 氦 (hài)

- **Chinese:** 氦 — *hài* · radical 气 (gas)  
- **Category:** Noble gas  
- **Atomic number:** 2 · **Atomic mass:** 4.0026  
- **State at room temperature:** gas  
- **Charge numbers — common:** +0  
- **Charge numbers — all known:** +0  
- **Discovered:** Pierre Janssen and Norman Lockyer, 1868  
- **Name means:** From Greek helios, 'sun'  
- **Abundance in Earth's crust:** 0.008 ppm  

**Description**

Helium is the second lightest element and the ultimate loner — it almost never joins up with other atoms, so it has no real compounds. It was discovered on the Sun before anyone found it on Earth, by studying sunlight through a prism. Today we collect it from underground gas wells.

**Fun facts**

- Helium was found on the Sun first — it is the only element discovered in space before Earth.
- Breathing a little helium makes your voice squeaky because sound travels faster through it.
- Helium is the only substance that never freezes solid, no matter how cold it gets, unless you also squeeze it.
- Below −271 °C liquid helium becomes a 'superfluid' that can climb up the walls of its container.
- Party balloons use only a tiny slice of our helium; most cools the giant magnets in hospital MRI scanners.

> **Note on compounds:** Helium is a noble gas and forms no stable everyday compounds. Chemists have only made fragile molecules like HeH+ (helium hydride) inside special equipment — it was also the very first molecule ever to form in the universe.
>
> - HeH⁺ — **Helium hydride ion**: Thought to be the first molecule ever made after the Big Bang; spotted in space in 2019.

- Boron carbide is so hard it is used in bulletproof vests and tank armor.
- Boron gobbles up neutrons, so it is used in nuclear reactor control rods to slow reactions down.
- Plants need a tiny amount of boron to grow — too little and their leaves curl up.

Fluorine is the greediest element of all — it grabs electrons from almost anything and reacts with nearly every other element. As a pure pale-yellow gas it is fiercely dangerous, yet once it is safely bonded it protects your teeth in toothpaste and stops eggs sticking to your frying pan.

## 10. Neon (Ne) · 氖 (nǎi)

- **Chinese:** 氖 — *nǎi* · radical 气 (gas)  
- **Category:** Noble gas  
- **Atomic number:** 10 · **Atomic mass:** 20.18  
- **State at room temperature:** gas  
- **Charge numbers — common:** +0  
- **Charge numbers — all known:** +0  
- **Discovered:** William Ramsay and Morris Travers, 1898  
- **Name means:** From Greek neos, 'new'  
- **Abundance in Earth's crust:** 0.005 ppm  

**Description**

Neon is a noble gas that refuses to react with anything at all, so it has no compounds. Its claim to fame is light: run electricity through neon and it glows a brilliant orange-red. That glow lit up the signs of cities all over the world for a hundred years.

**Fun facts**

- Neon signs only glow orange-red; other colors come from different gases or coatings.
- Neon was named 'new' by a 12-year-old — the discoverer's son suggested it.
- Neon is the fifth most common element in the universe but very rare on Earth.
- A neon light has no filament to burn out, so signs can last for decades.
- Liquid neon is a far better refrigerant than liquid helium for its volume.

> **Note on compounds:** Neon is the least reactive element known. It forms no chemical compounds at all — not even fragile laboratory ones. Scientists have only ever coaxed it into loose 'clusters' held together by weak attraction, such as Ne–Ne pairs in ultra-cold experiments.
